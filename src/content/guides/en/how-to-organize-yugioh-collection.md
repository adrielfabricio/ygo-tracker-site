Organizing a Yu-Gi-Oh! collection comes down to three questions: **what you own**, **what each deck needs** and **how much is left to spend**. Once those answers live in one place, you stop buying doubles and walk into the shop knowing exactly what to ask for.

This guide walks through setting that up step by step. You can follow it with pen and paper, a spreadsheet or an app.

## 1. Decide what each card is for

Before sorting anything, separate two goals:

- **Decks to play:** what matters is owning the right number of copies of every card on the decklist.
- **Sets to complete:** what matters is owning every card in a set, often in a specific rarity.

The same card can serve both, but mixing the two goals is what usually breaks the count. A copy sleeved in a deck is not in the set binder, and the other way around.

## 2. Organize the physical cards

A simple split that works for most collectors:

- **Binders with 9-pocket pages** for cards you want to see and complete, sorted by set or archetype.
- **Deck boxes with sleeves** for decks that are ready to play.
- **Storage boxes** for spare copies, sorted by card type (monsters, spells and traps) or by archetype.

Inside each deck, keep the **Main Deck**, **Extra Deck** and **Side Deck** apart. It makes checking that everything is there before a tournament much faster.

## 3. Take inventory of what you own

For every card, record at least:

- **Name**;
- **Set code**, the one printed under the artwork, such as LOB-EN001;
- **Rarity**;
- **Quantity**.

Set and rarity matter because the same card can have several printings at very different prices.

This is where improvised methods tend to fail. The screenshot gets lost in the camera roll, the spreadsheet is out of date by the second week, and the note you messaged to yourself disappears in the chat. An inventory only works if it is quick to update the moment a card arrives.

## 4. Work out which cards each deck is missing

Keep the basic deck-building rules in mind:

- **Main Deck:** 40 to 60 cards;
- **Extra Deck:** up to 15 cards;
- **Side Deck:** up to 15 cards;
- **At most 3 copies** of any card, within the Forbidden & Limited List.

With the decklist in hand, the math is simple: **missing = copies needed − copies owned**.

| Card                        | Needed | Owned | Missing |
| --------------------------- | ------ | ----- | ------- |
| Ash Blossom & Joyous Spring | 3      | 1     | 2       |
| Infinite Impermanence       | 3      | 3     | 0       |
| Effect Veiler               | 2      | 0     | 2       |

Do this for the Main, Extra and Side Deck. The total of the "Missing" column is your shopping list for that deck.

## 5. Estimate what it will cost

For every missing card, note a **reference price**: what you found at one or two shops, in the printing and rarity you plan to buy. Multiply by the number missing and add everything up to get an estimate for the deck.

Prices move often, so treat the number as an estimate and check again before you buy. Even so, having it helps you decide which deck to finish first.

## 6. Keep names in more than one language

If you play with cards printed in another language, keep both names. Many cards were released in Portuguese with translated names: "Dragão Branco de Olhos Azuis" is "Blue-Eyes White Dragon". Shops, databases and online decklists usually use the English name, so having both avoids mix-ups and buying the wrong card.

## 7. Take the list to the shop

When you go shopping, bring only what is missing: name, quantity and, if it matters, the printing you want. To swap decklists with friends, the **YDKe** format is a URL that encodes the whole deck and is accepted by many simulators and decklist sites.
